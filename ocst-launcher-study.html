Navigation study

Four ways into the work

Users can't find how to start a field visit or an O&M verification. The cause isn't menu depth — it's that both navigations are built entirely from nouns while users arrive with verbs. Four structures, judged against the five journeys that matter.

The structures

The recommendation is first; the four single-structure options behind it are the exploration that led there. Tap anything to see the path it produces. A, B and C are launchers — lists of verbs. D is different in kind: it uses where you're standing to answer which record.

The idea

Both panels, always. The hero tier currently holds a card called Quick Actions containing four navigation links and no actions — that's the space this takes.

Neither panel is hidden by role, so a one-person city keeps every tool. Priority is expressed by order, and order follows the viewport: touch and narrow puts field first, wide puts desk first.

Why the field panel starts locked

A map panel isn't free the way a verb list is — it wants location permission and map tiles on every home page load. Rendering it eagerly means every desk user gets a prompt they'll deny once and then be stuck with.

So it opens as a placeholder and asks on tap. That also gives an honest empty state for someone twenty miles from the nearest BMP, where the answer is a search box, not an apology.

Open questions

  • Viewport is a proxy for context, and it will be wrong sometimes — a crew on a laptop in a truck, a manager on an iPad at a desk. Worth a remembered preference if the proxy annoys people.
  • O&M verification and delineations are genuinely both field and desk work, so they appear in both panels. Same destination, different context.
  • Provisional review shows a count. Counts that can be zero need a designed empty state, not a stray "0".

The idea

GPS answers which of these few, not which one. So the ranked list is the thing you act on — full-width rows, one primary action each, chosen from what that record actually needs. The map sits above for orientation and to confirm you're looking at the right thing.

Every row's action is the verb for that record's current state: a BMP with no visit offers Start visit, one mid-visit offers Continue, one with no delineation offers Delineate.

No typing, no grid search, no picker. The find step is gone rather than relocated.

Already built

  • zoom-to-my-location-control — reusable Leaflet control, already on the BMP list map.
  • hybrid-map-grid — reusable map + grid with two-way selection sync.
  • The delineation map already does map-selection-to-action-panel. This generalises that.

The missing piece is an actions slot on hybrid-map-grid; it has one for layers only.

Risks worth testing

  • GPS is 5–10 m at best, worse under canopy. Never auto-select — always offer candidates.
  • Needs HTTPS and a permission prompt, and fails indoors. Manual search stays as fallback.
  • Map tiles over cellular are heavier than a list. The list should render before the map does.
  • Dense marker clusters are hard to hit with a finger — the reason the list, not the map, is the action surface.

Why D is different in kind

Three of the five tasks are scoped to a record, and a launcher can only relocate the question of which one. A field visit needs a BMP; a verification needs a WQMP; a delineation needs a BMP. A, B and C all replace menu, then grid, then row action with a search-as-you-type picker — better, but the user still has to identify and name the record. That's why those variants land at two or three taps and not one.

D removes the question instead of moving it. Standing next to the thing is a stronger identifier than typing its name, and it's the one piece of context a tablet in the field has that a desk browser doesn't. This is the only variant where the find step disappears rather than shrinks.

It is also the least speculative. The geolocation control, the map-and-grid component with two-way selection, and the row actions all exist today; the delineation map already turns map selection into an action panel. What's missing is an actions slot on the shared component.

The worst journey still isn't on this screen. Adding BMPs to a WQMP costs five clicks per BMP because you cannot create a child record from the parent you're viewing, and no amount of geolocation helps someone at a desk with a PDF. That's fixed on the WQMP page. Desk pain is in-context; field pain is wayfinding.

Recommendation

Both panels — a desk verb list and a location-aware field panel — in the hero tier, ordered by viewport. The two user groups fail for different reasons, so no single structure was ever going to serve both. The desk group's problem is that verbs don't exist anywhere in the navigation; the field group's problem is being asked to name a record they're standing next to. One panel each.

Within the field panel, D subsumes C: a record you have work open on is just a nearby record whose verb is Continue rather than Start. And the worst journey of the five stays off this screen entirely — adding BMPs to a WQMP is fixed on the WQMP page with an in-context action, because no amount of geolocation helps someone at a desk with a PDF.

Sequence. The two dead-end fixes first — a launcher on the O&M Verifications list and on Field Records — because they're days of work, they address two of the three reported pain points, and they're independent of everything else here. Then the desk verb panel, which is plain routing and needs no new components. Then the actions slot on hybrid-map-grid, which is the whole engineering cost of the field panel. Then the WQMP in-context fix.

Click counts for the variants are projections from the traced routes, not measurements. The baseline column is traced from the actual code; the variant columns are estimates and should be treated as such. The field panel's estimates additionally assume location permission is already granted — first run costs an extra tap, and indoors it degrades to search.
